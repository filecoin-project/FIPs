# FIP-0122: Size the miner actor's reward-derived charges (faults) by the consensus weight

## Simple Summary

Size the charges a storage provider pays from the share of the block reward it actually receives after the Solstice split, rather than from the whole minted amount. Initial pledge and the PreCommit Deposit do not change.

## Abstract

[FIP-0118](https://github.com/filecoin-project/FIPs/blob/master/FIPS/fip-0118.md) splits each block reward into streams. An elected leader receives `allocation.miner = floor(w1 * BR)`, where $`w_1`$ is the weight of the implicit consensus stream, ramping down from 95% to a floor of 50%.

The reward actor's `this_epoch_reward` is unchanged by that split. It is still the whole minted value for the epoch, and both the reward actor and the power actor pass its smoothed estimate on unchanged. The miner actor sizes the fault penalties from that estimate, with no $`w_1`$ anywhere. Those penalties are defined as multiples of what a sector earns, so each now overstates an SP's income by $`1/w_1`$, reaching a factor of two at the floor of the ramp.

This proposal carries the consensus weight to the miner actor and applies it where such a charge is computed. `this_epoch_reward` keeps its present meaning and value, so the reward actor's issuance accounting is untouched. Initial pledge, the PreCommit Deposit and the termination-fee constants do not change, and no state migration is needed.

## Change Motivation

Solstice lowers the block reward paid to storage providers, and lowers it gradually: the consensus weight starts at 95% and ramps down to a floor of 50%. A provider's income per win falls with it, by design.

The charges a provider pays do not fall with it, because they are still computed from the whole block reward. The fault fee states its own intent in the actor code, where the projection period exists so that "a miner pays back at least the expected block reward earned since the last successful PoSt", in the code's own words ([`monies.rs`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/actors/miner/src/monies.rs#L72-L80)). Computed from the whole reward while the provider collects $`w_1`$ of it, the fee takes back $`1/w_1`$ times what the sector earned, which is 3.51 days of income for every faulty day before the split and 7.02 once the weight reaches its floor.

The overpayment widens as the ramp advances, and it widens for every provider at once. FIP-0118 is silent on it. The words "penalties are assessed as today" refer to the `penalty` argument of `AwardBlockReward` and point at Section 2.4, and the FIP does not discuss the fault fee anywhere. The effect follows from leaving `this_epoch_reward` whole, so this proposal decides the question rather than inferring an answer from FIP-0118.

The continued-fault fee and the invalid-WindowPoSt penalty are both projections of a faulty sector's earnings. This is the largest continuous exposure, charged per sector per day at the weight in force that day.

Over the last 365 days the fault fee came to about 214,000 FIL, 0.93% of minted rewards, from the Filecoin Data Portal's `storage_provider_cron_penalty_assessed_fil`, which Lily computes from the fault fee function rather than from traced burns. Scaling it at $`w_1 = 0.5`$ would return at most about 107,000 FIL a year to providers, against roughly 11.5M FIL of reduced receipts over the same period.


## Specification

Let $`w_1(t)`$ be the weight of the implicit reward stream at epoch $`t`$, expressed over `DENOM` $`= 10^{18}`$. The stream invariants allow at most one implicit stream, so this is a single value. It is identified by being the implicit stream, not by its position in the stream list.

### 1. Carrying the consensus weight to the miner actor

The miner actor obtains the reward estimate by two routes, and both of them need the weight.

On the direct route the miner actor asks the reward actor, which replies with [`ThisEpochRewardReturn`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/runtime/src/builtin/reward/mod.rs#L12-L18). Three paths on this route use the weight: the WindowPoSt dispute, which prices the invalid-WindowPoSt penalty; `TerminateSectors`, which prices the fault fee that feeds the termination fee; and the exported `MaxTerminationFee` method, which prices the same quantity for a caller. Pre-commit, prove-commit, replica update, sector upgrade and the consensus-fault report take the same reply but size nothing this proposal scales.

On the cron route the miner actor is called by the power actor, which supplies the estimate inside [`DeferredCronEventParams`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/actors/miner/src/types.rs#L99-L105). The power actor takes the value from the reward actor and forwards it ([`power/src/lib.rs`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/actors/power/src/lib.rs#L401-L404)). This route covers deadline advance and early-termination processing, which is where the continued-fault fee is charged.

Both structs shall carry $`w_1(t)`$ alongside the estimate they already carry, and the power actor shall forward the value it receives. Neither route gains a new message.

`ThisEpochReward` evaluates the implicit stream's weight from the stored records at the epoch of the call and does not apply queued writes. If the streams block does not decode, or its records fail validation, the weight reads as zero and the floor in section 2 applies, which matches the award, which pays gas only in that state. A store error aborts, so every node reaches the same verdict from the same bytes. `this_epoch_reward` and `this_epoch_reward_smoothed` keep their present meaning and value.

### 2. The weight used for sizing

Charges shall be sized with $`w_1(t)`$ itself. There is no floor.

Stream governance can set the consensus weight to zero, and can remove the implicit stream altogether, in which case $`w_1(t)`$ reads as zero and the fault penalties in section 3 are sized at zero with it, apart from the 20 FIL dispute base. See the Design Rationale for why that is the intended behaviour rather than a case to be guarded against.

Write $`BR(t,d)`$ for the existing projection of a sector's reward over $`d`$ epochs and define

$`\widehat{BR}(t,d) = w_1(t)\cdot BR(t,d)`$

Each product is taken in big integers and divided with floor, as FIP-0118 specifies for the award split.

### 3. Fault penalties

The following shall be computed from $`\widehat{BR}`$ in place of $`BR`$:

| Quantity | Definition today | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continued-fault fee, $`FF(t)`$ | $`BR(t,\ 3.51\text{d})`$ | `pledge_penalty_for_continued_fault` |
| Invalid-WindowPoSt penalty | $`BR(t,\ 5.51\text{d}) + 20`$ FIL | `pledge_penalty_for_invalid_windowpost` |

The projection periods, the two-day margin between them and the 20 FIL dispute base do not change.

### 4. Unchanged

- **Initial pledge**, both the storage-pledge term `IPBase` and the circulating-supply term `AdditionalIP`.
- **The PreCommit Deposit**, $`BR(t,\ 20\text{d})`$ via `pre_commit_deposit_for_power`.
- **The termination fee's own constants:** the 8.5% percentage, the 2% floor, the 1.05 fault-fee floor and `TERMINATION_LIFETIME_CAP = 140`.
- **The FIP-0100 daily fee**, `daily_proof_fee`, and the cap applied to it. The fee is a share of circulating supply, and `DAILY_FEE_BLOCK_REWARD_CAP_DENOM` stays at 2. See the Design Rationale for why the cap is left alone.
- **The termination fee computed from those constants** does read the scaled fault fee through the 1.05 floor. Backwards Compatibility shows that branch is about nine times below the 2% pledge floor, so the fee does not move at current values.
- **The values and schedule of** $`w_0, w_1, w_2`$, which remain governed as FIP-0118 specifies.
- **The reward actor's issuance accounting**, including `this_epoch_reward`, `FilMined` and the circulating-supply inputs.
- **The consensus-fault penalty.** It deters a consensus attack rather than clawing back earnings. The factor of 5 has no documented rationale in FIP-0011, in the 2020 economics paper or in the actor code, and an equivocator's gain is not bounded by the block reward, so there is no ratio that scaling would preserve.
- **The consensus-fault reporter reward**, `BlockReward / 4` from FIP-0011. It is an incentive to report, denominated in block reward for convenience, and there is no reason for it to track an SP's share of the reward.
- **The proofs, their parameters and the sealing process.**

The lower-bound termination penalty, `pledge_penalty_for_termination_lower_bound`, is reward-derived but has no caller outside its own tests. It is not scaled, and removing it is suggested as a separate cleanup.

## Design Rationale

**What is in scope, and why.** The charges scaled here are the ones whose size is a projection of a sector's block reward: the fault penalties. The termination fee and the daily fee are charges too and are both excluded, because neither is a reward projection: the termination fee is a percentage of pledge, and the daily fee is a share of circulating supply. Pledge and the PreCommit Deposit are reward projections, and are excluded as collateral, returned rather than spent.

**Why initial pledge is out.** `IPBase` is a reward projection, and the actor code calls it "a multiple of the reward expected to be earned by newly-committed power", so the reasoning that scales the fault fee would reach it too. `AdditionalIP` is a share of circulating supply and is not a reward projection at all. Pledge is excluded because pledge is collateral rather than a charge: locked against the sector, returned, and there to stand behind the charges rather than to be one. Scaling the fault fee without scaling pledge widens that cover instead of narrowing it, since `IPBase` covers 5.7 days of fault fees at $`w_1 = 1`$ and 11.4 days at $`w_1 = 0.5`$. The termination fee is a percentage of pledge and follows it out of scope, and total FIL locked is the aggregate of pledge and does not move either.

**Why the PreCommit Deposit is out.** It is collateral on the same reading as pledge, and a lock rather than a charge: released at prove-commit into the initial pledge, burned only when a pre-commit expires unproven, and held for days rather than for a sector's lifetime. What it deters is also priced off-chain. Abandoning a pre-commit to re-draw seal randomness, or occupying the pre-commit queue, pays off in sealing compute and chain state, and those payoffs do not fall when $`w_1`$ falls.

**Why the FIP-0100 daily-fee cap is left alone.** An earlier draft scaled the cap's input for consistency, since it reads the same estimate. Two things argue against it. The cap binds only when the daily fee approaches half a sector's block reward, and the fee sits around 3.4% of block reward today, so scaling it changes nothing a provider would notice. More importantly, the cap is $`\min(\text{estimated day reward}/2,\ \text{daily fee})`$, so a scaled input that reached zero would take the daily fee payable to zero with it, for every sector rather than only faulty ones. FIP-0100 denominates that fee in circulating supply precisely so that it does not track block reward, and scaling its cap would introduce that coupling here.

**Why there is no floor on the weight.** Stream governance can set the consensus weight to zero and can remove the implicit stream, so the case has to be decided rather than left implicit. An earlier draft floored the sizing weight at 0.5, the floor of the published schedule. That was rejected for an asymmetry: FIP-0118 can move $`w_1`$ through a governed contract write, while undoing a floor written into the actors would need a network upgrade, so the floor would outlive the schedule it was copied from.

Taking the weight as it comes means that at $`w_1 = 0`$ the fault fee is zero. That is the intended reading rather than a gap. The fee exists so that an SP pays back what it earned while faulty, and at a zero consensus weight it earned nothing from consensus to pay back. What a faulty sector still faces does not depend on the weight: it loses its power on detection, it keeps paying the FIP-0100 daily fee, which is a share of circulating supply and is untouched by this proposal, the invalid-WindowPoSt penalty keeps its 20 FIL base, and termination still costs at least 2% of pledge.

**Why the weight is applied at the point of use.** Scaling `this_epoch_reward` at the source would reach every consumer indiscriminately, pledge and the deposit included, and would break the reward actor's issuance accounting and the invariant pinning it to `compute_reward(...)`. Carrying the weight as a separate value keeps the issuance record exact and makes the set of affected charges explicit.

**Why the consensus weight alone, and not the consensus and service weights together.** The charges in scope project what a sector earns, and the service stream is paid to registered Service Orchestrators rather than to the elected leader. An SP's receipts from a win are $`w_1 \cdot BR`$ and nothing more.

**One change, one mechanism.** Section 3 is the whole of the behaviour change, and section 1 is the whole of its cost.

## Backwards Compatibility

Two replies between actors carry one more value than before. Both are positional lists rather than named records, so a reader identifies each value by its place and cannot skip one it does not recognise. Every reader of `ThisEpochRewardReturn` and of `DeferredCronEventParams` has to be updated. The impact falls on node implementations, since both of those methods sit at low method numbers and are not reachable from FEVM. Chain state is unaffected, and `ThisEpochRewardReturn` has lost a value once before, so there is precedent for versioning the change.

One exported method changes its answer without changing its shape. `MaxTerminationFee`, added by FIP-0098 and dispatched through FRC-42, prices a sector's fault fee and returns the termination fee derived from it, but it evaluates that formula at the maximum sector age, so its result is 8.5% of pledge unless the fault fee rises above about 8.1% of pledge, and the fault fee is roughly 38 times below that today. The method should read the scaled fault fee for consistency with the termination path, but its answer does not move on current values.

No state migration is required. Every charge in scope is computed when it is charged and takes the new value as soon as the change is active. Nothing recorded against existing sectors changes, since pledge is untouched and no sector's stored state carries any of these values.

Termination fees do not move at all on current values. The minimum is $`\max(0.02\,IP,\ 1.05\,FF)`$, and the 2% branch is already far the larger of the two. For one 32 GiB sector, read from current reward and power state: at 1x, pledge 0.267 FIL, so 2% of pledge is $`5.34\times10^{-3}`$ FIL against $`1.05\,FF = 5.95\times10^{-4}`$ FIL; at 10x, pledge 2.669 FIL, so $`5.34\times10^{-2}`$ against $`5.95\times10^{-3}`$. As a check against chain state, a recently onboarded 10x sector, SP f03573579 sector 341139, carries an `InitialPledge` of 2.669 FIL. The pledge floor is about nine times the fault-fee floor before any scaling, so lowering the fault fee never brings the fault-fee branch into play.

## Test Cases

1. With the implicit stream at 100%, every charge in scope is unchanged from its current value.
2. At the floor of the ramp, the continued-fault fee for a given faulty power is half the value computed from the unsplit estimate, and equals one projection period of what the sector's wins pay.
3. The invalid-WindowPoSt penalty scales the same way, with the 20 FIL dispute base added after scaling rather than before.
4. An SP charged the continued-fault fee through the cron route is charged the same amount as the direct route would give for the same power and weight.
5. With the consensus weight set to zero, and separately with the implicit stream removed, the continued-fault fee is zero, the invalid-WindowPoSt penalty is the 20 FIL base alone, and the FIP-0100 daily fee charged at deadline advance is unchanged.
6. A zero weight divides nothing. The scaling is a multiplication, and the projection it multiplies divides by the network QAP estimate rather than by the weight, with its own zero branch at [`monies.rs`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/actors/miner/src/monies.rs#L97-L115). No quantity in scope takes the weight, the scaled estimate or the fault fee as a divisor.
7. A zero fault fee is applied without error. [`apply_penalty`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/actors/miner/src/state.rs#L800-L807) rejects only negative values, so a zero fee adds nothing to fee debt, and the deadline advance that charges it completes and still applies the daily fee and the power delta.
8. Scaling never turns a strictly positive accounting value into zero. The variant that clamps the projection at one atto, `expected_reward_for_power_clamped_at_atto_fil`, exists because the PreCommit Deposit and `IPBase` are used as indicators of miner activity and must stay positive. Both are out of scope, and they are its only callers, so no in-scope charge reads the clamped form.
9. At a zero weight the termination path and `MaxTerminationFee` return the same values as at any other weight for a sector on the 2% pledge floor. The `1.05 * FF` branch evaluates to zero, `max(0.02 * IP, 0)` is the pledge floor, and nothing divides by the fault fee.
10. The PreCommit Deposit and initial pledge are unchanged under every weight schedule, including zero.
11. A sector whose termination fee is set by the 2% pledge floor is unaffected by the weight, and `MaxTerminationFee` prices its fault fee at the same weight as the termination path while still returning 8.5% of pledge for that sector, since it evaluates at the maximum sector age.
12. A sector onboarded through NI-PoRep, which has no pre-commit and no deposit, pays the same scaled fault fee as one onboarded through pre-commit and prove-commit.
13. The reward actor's stored `this_epoch_reward` still equals `compute_reward(...)` exactly, and its issuance counters are unaffected.
14. Reading the weight when the implicit stream is absent, and when the streams block fails to decode or its records fail validation, yields zero on both routes without aborting `ThisEpochReward` or the power actor's deferred cron.

## Security Considerations

Every charge in scope is reduced and none is raised, so the question for each is whether it still covers what it was set to cover.

**Fault penalties.** The fee exists so that holding power without storing the data is not profitable. The comparison is between the fee and what the sector earns while faulty, and both sides move by $`w_1`$, so the ratio is preserved exactly. The calibration that held before Solstice is restored rather than altered.

**A zero consensus weight.** With no floor, a zero weight takes the fault fee to zero. Three things keep a faulty sector costly without reference to the weight. It loses its power when the fault is detected, since the power delta on a missed PoSt is $`\text{unproven} - \text{newly faulty}`$ ([`partition_state.rs`](https://github.com/filecoin-project/builtin-actors/blob/dbba8a0/actors/miner/src/partition_state.rs#L628-L636)). It keeps paying the FIP-0100 daily fee, which this proposal does not touch, including its cap, and which a faulty sector still owes because faulty power stays inside live power. And termination still costs at least 2% of pledge, since pledge is not scaled. The invalid-WindowPoSt penalty also keeps its 20 FIL base, which is added after scaling.

Zero is a legal value for every quantity in scope rather than a degenerate one. The actor code already distinguishes the charges that may be zero from the accounting values that may not: `expected_reward_for_power_clamped_at_atto_fil` exists so that the PreCommit Deposit and `IPBase` stay strictly positive and can be read as indicators of miner activity, and those two are the only callers. The fault penalties read the unclamped projection. Nothing in scope divides by the weight, by the scaled estimate or by the fault fee, so a zero weight produces zero charges rather than an error. The test cases above pin each of these down.

**Termination.** Pledge is unchanged, so the 8.5% branch is unchanged and FIP-0098's calibration is left intact. The $`1.05\,FF`$ floor falls with the fault fee, but it already sits about nine times below the 2% pledge floor, so the larger of the two is unaffected and the invariant that terminating is never cheaper than remaining faulty continues to hold.

**Pre-commit.** The deposit is unchanged. FIP-0119's malformed-replica condition compares the deposit with the expected gain from sealing a bad replica and under the split that gain is `w1` of what it was, so the margin FIP-0119 reports at the 10-year cap, 5.6x, grows as the weight falls.

## Incentive Considerations

Providers currently pay fault and dispute penalties sized to a reward they receive only a fraction of, and that fraction is scheduled to shrink further. This proposal returns each charge to the ratio it held relative to provider income before Solstice, and no charge rises.

The effect is largest where exposure is continuous, since a faulty sector's daily penalty falls to one projection period of its real income.

Onboarding capital is unaffected. Neither pledge nor the PreCommit Deposit changes, so the FIL a provider must lock to onboard a sector is what it is today.

## Product Considerations

Fault cost returns to a stated multiple of what a sector earns, rather than a multiple that grows as the consensus weight falls. Tooling that projects fault cost from the reward estimate has to apply the consensus weight, or it will overstate that cost by up to a factor of two. Tooling that projects pledge, the PreCommit Deposit or termination cost needs no change.

## Implementation

One value added to `ThisEpochRewardReturn` and one to `DeferredCronEventParams`, populated by the reward actor from the evaluated weight schedule and forwarded by the power actor; one multiplication in the miner actor at the sites listed in section 3; the pre-commit and pledge sites left alone. No migration, no new inter-actor message, and no change to the reward actor's issuance accounting.

One cost is worth measuring before implementation. The weight sits in the block behind `streams_root`, which `ThisEpochReward` does not load today, so carrying it adds a block load to every call that reads the epoch reward, including the pledge and deposit calls that make no use of it. The gas cost of that load has not been measured.
